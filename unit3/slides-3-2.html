        Radius · Textbook §3.2.1

          Measuring atomic radius

          Covalent radius

          A tiny unit

          1 picometer (pm) = 10−12 m. Radii run from 31 pm (hydrogen) to 244 pm (cesium).

          One data set

          An atom has no hard edge, so its size depends on how it is measured.

          Compare one kind of radius from one source. This unit: covalent radii, Cordero and coworkers, 2008. Noble gases are left out: they form few or no compounds.

          1. No edge · 1 min. The electron cloud fades out gradually. Chemists measure the distance between bonded nuclei, which X-ray crystallography gives very precisely.
          2. Unit · 1 min. A cesium atom is about 488 pm wide, so about 2 million fit side by side in 1 mm (worked example in the reading).
          3. One data set · 1 min. A radius from a metal crystal can differ by tens of pm. Mixing sources creates false trends.
          4. Check · 1 min. Misconception: the radius is the whole distance between nuclei.

              Model · Textbook §3.2.2 · SEP 1(G)

                Three factors in the model

                1 · Nuclear charge

                More protons pull harder on every electron.

                2 · Energy level

                Outer electrons in a higher level are farther from the nucleus.

                3 · Shielding

                Inner electrons cancel part of the pull. Sodium: 10 inner electrons shield 1 outer electron from 11 protons.

                Physical causes

                "An atom wants a full outer level" is shorthand. The real cause is the pull of the nucleus on the outer electrons.

                1. Nuclear charge · 1 min. Click Na, then Cl. The dashed circle shows the element chosen before; the sentence compares the two.
                2. Energy level · 1 min. Switch to Group 1 and click Li, Na, K.
                3. Shielding · 1 min. The shaded disk holds the inner electrons. Its labels give the electrons in each inner level.
                4. Physical cause · 1 min. This fulfills the promise from Unit 2. The model, not a desire, explains the patterns.

                  Period 4, groups 13 to 17: Ga 122, Ge 120, As 119, Se 120, Br 120 pm. The model predicts a steady drop. The reason involves d electrons, beyond this course.

                  4. Check · 1 min. Misconception: the added electron goes into an inner level.

                      Ionization · Textbook §3.2.3 · SEP 1(G)

                        Ionization energy

                        First ionization energy

                        The energy to remove one electron from each atom in 1 mol of gaseous atoms.

                        Equation

                        Na(g) + 496 kJ → Na+(g) + e−, for 1 mol of atoms. Unit: kJ/mol.

                        Always takes energy

                        Every electron is attracted to the nucleus. Helium needs the most of any element: 2372 kJ/mol.

                        Try it

                        Remove an electron from each period 3 element. Which needs the most energy? Then try Group 1.

                        1. Define · 1 min. Gaseous, so each atom is separate. It is not the energy of a reaction with water or air.
                        2. Equation · 1 min. Energy is on the left: it is absorbed. Values from NIST, rounded to whole kJ/mol.
                        3. Energy needed · 30 s. Return to prediction 3: helium, not iron.
                        4. Try · 2 min. Students call out the order. A bar appears only after the electron is removed.

                          Ionization · Textbook §3.2.3

                            Explaining ionization energy

                            Across a period →

                            More protons, same shielding. The outer electrons are held more tightly, so removing one takes more energy.

                            Down a group ↓

                            A higher level, more shielding. The outer electron is held less tightly, so removing it takes less energy. K 419 < Na 496 kJ/mol.

                            Your turn

                            Magnesium: 738 kJ/mol. Calcium: 590 kJ/mol. Which loses an electron more easily, and by how much?

                            Answer

                            Subtract738 − 590 = 148 kJ/mol

                            ExplainCalcium: its outer electron is in level 4, farther out and more shielded.

                            1. Across · 1 min. Same reasoning as radius: stronger pull, smaller atom, tighter hold.
                            2. Down · 1 min. Lesson 3.3 connects the easy loss of potassium's electron to how strongly it reacts.
                            3. Your turn · 2 min. Calculators out. Students write a number and one reason.
                            4. Answer · 1 min. Look for "farther" and "shielded" in the reason, not "wants to lose".

                            Ionization · Textbook §3.2.3

                              Noble gas to alkali metal

                              Three pairs

                              He → Li, Ne → Na, Ar → K. Each noble gas is followed by a steep drop.

                              Watch

                              Ne 2081 − Na 496 = 1585 kJ/mol

                              Why

                              Sodium's outer electron is the first in level 3, outside 10 inner electrons: farther out and well shielded.

                              1. Pairs · 1 min. Click the bars to read values: He 2372, Li 520, Ne 2081, Na 496, Ar 1521, K 419 kJ/mol.
                              2. Watch · 1 min. Ask students to compute the Ar to K drop: 1102 kJ/mol.
                              3. Why · 1 min. A new period means a new energy level.
                              4. Check · 1 min. Misconception: potassium has fewer protons than argon.

                              Honors · Textbook §3.2.3

                                Honors: two dips in period 2

                                B 801 < Be 900

                                Boron's outer electron is the first in 2p: higher in energy than 2s and partly shielded by the 2s electrons.

                                O 1314 < N 1402

                                Oxygen's fourth 2p electron shares an orbital. The paired electrons repel, so one is easier to remove.

                                1. Boron · 1 min. Optional for on-level classes. Uses sublevels from Lesson 2.5.
                                2. Oxygen · 1 min. Draw the orbital boxes for N (three single 2p electrons) and O (one pair). Period 3 has the same dips: Al 578 < Mg 738 and S 1000 < P 1012 kJ/mol. The general trend still holds: argon is far above sodium.
                                3. Check · 1 min. Misconception: oxygen has fewer protons than nitrogen.

                                  Rank and predict · Textbook §3.2.4

                                    Ranking atoms by position

                                    Same period

                                    Farther right: smaller radius, usually higher ionization energy.

                                    Na: 166 pm, 496 kJ/mol

                                    Cl: 102 pm, 1251 kJ/mol

                                    Same group

                                    Lower down: larger radius, lower ionization energy.

                                              Wrap-up · Exit ticket · SEP 3(A)

                                                Answer the driving question

                                                Why do atomic size and the energy to remove an electron change in a regular pattern across the periodic table? Write your answer in your notebook.

                                                Word bank
                                                atomic radiusionization energynuclear chargeenergy levelinner electronsshieldingperiodic trend

                                                Claim

                                                One sentence that answers the question.

                                                1. The patterns repeat because…
                                                2. Size and ionization energy both depend on…

                                                Sample claim

                                                Both properties depend on how strongly the nucleus pulls on the outer electrons, and that pull changes in the same way in every period.

                                                Evidence

                                                Data from a period and from a group.

                                                1. Across period 3…
                                                2. Down group 1…

                                                Sample evidence

                                                Across period 3, the radius falls from 166 pm (Na) to 102 pm (Cl) and ionization energy rises from 496 to 1521 kJ/mol (Ar). Down group 1, the radius grows from 128 to 244 pm and ionization energy falls from 520 to 376 kJ/mol.

                                                Reasoning

                                                Use the three factors of the model.

                                                1. Across a period, protons… while inner electrons…
                                                2. Down a group, each element adds…

                                                Sample reasoning

                                                Across a period, protons increase while the inner electrons stay the same, so the pull grows: smaller atoms, tighter hold. Down a group, each element adds an energy level and more shielding, so the outer electron is farther out and easier to remove.

                                                Self-check: mark each part your answer meets, then compare with the sample.

                                                1. Claim · 2 min. One sentence. Push for "pull of the nucleus" rather than "atoms want."
                                                2. Evidence · 3 min. Must name elements with values for both properties.
                                                3. Reasoning · 3 min. Must use all three factors.
                                                4. Self-check · 2 min. Students self-check, then you show the sample. Score 0 to 2 per part.
