Counting by weighing

The bank

A sealed bag of nickels on the bank's scale.

Zero the scale

First, zero the scale with the empty bag. Now it reads the coins alone.

Predict

The scale reads 1,250.0 g. How many nickels?

Divide

Mass of one nickel5.000 g

1,250.0 g ÷ 5.000 g per nickel. The grams cancel.

Count

250 nickels. Check: 250 × 5.000 g = 1,250.0 g.

Rule

If every object has the same mass, divide the total by one object's mass. The grams cancel. A pure number is left.

Check

A roll of nickels has a mass of 200.0 g. How many nickels is that?

  1. A40
  2. B1,000
  3. C200

A. 200.0 g ÷ 5.000 g per nickel = 40 nickels.

Zeroing (taring) in words: the scale reads 0.0 g with the empty bag on it, so it then shows the mass of the coins alone. The grams cancel, leaving a pure number of nickels.

The mole

One mole: 6.022 × 1023 particles. The symbol is mol.

Exactly

Since May 20, 2019: exactly 6.02214076 × 1023. We round it to 6.022 × 1023.

Number or constant

Avogadro's number has no unit. The Avogadro constant is the same number per mole: 6.022 × 1023 mol−1.

Rule

One mole contains exactly 6.02214076 × 1023 particles. In calculations we round it to 6.022 × 1023. A mole is a count, not a mass.

Check

A mole is a…

  1. Acount of particles: 6.022 × 1023 of them
  2. Bmass of a substance in grams
  3. Cvolume of a substance

A. Like a dozen, a mole is a number of things. A very large one.

"Amount of substance" is the SI quantity; the mole is its unit. The rounded value 6.022 × 10²³ is the exact number written with fewer digits, not a measurement. N_A is read "N sub A".

Who found the number

1811: Avogadro's idea. Equal gas volumes hold equal numbers of particles. He never knew the number.

1865: Loschmidt made the first estimate of the size of molecules.

1890s: Ostwald made the word Mol a unit.

1909: Jean Perrin measured it several ways and named it after Avogadro. Nobel Prize, 1926.

1971: the mole became an SI base unit. Old definition: the atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12.

Predict: today, is the number measured, or fixed by definition?

Voted November 16, 2018. Exact since May 20, 2019. No longer measured.

Rule

Since May 20, 2019 the number is exact: 6.02214076 × 1023. It is no longer measured.

The reading's section "How the number was found". The 1971 definition (the atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12) is "a definition that older books still print". The silicon spheres of the Avogadro project come next, on slide 13.

Counting the atoms in a silicon sphere

One crystal

The sphere is one crystal. Its atoms repeat in identical cubes.

One cube

X-rays measure one cube's edge: 543.102 pm. Each cube holds 8 atoms.

One atom

The density gives the mass of one cube, so of one atom.

The count

Mass of one mole of silicon ÷ mass of one atom = 6.022 × 1023.

Rule

The atoms of a crystal repeat in identical cubes. Count one cube and measure its size. Then the whole sphere is counted.

Check

In the Avogadro project, a silicon sphere was used to…

  1. Acount its atoms: mass, density, atom spacing
  2. Bdefine the kilogram by its size
  3. Ccompare silicon and carbon atoms

A. They gave the value fixed in 2019.

Honors (notes only, never assessed). The real spheres were enriched to more than 99.99% silicon-28: natural silicon is a mix of silicon-28, -29 and -30, and every uncertainty in those abundances becomes an uncertainty in the count; the other isotopes were measured with a mass spectrometer. The model's arithmetic with natural silicon: one cube has an edge of 543.102 pm, so a volume of (5.43102 × 10⁻⁸ cm)³ = 1.6019 × 10⁻²² cm³; × 2.329 g/cm³ = 3.7308 × 10⁻²² g per cube; ÷ 8 atoms = 4.6635 × 10⁻²³ g per atom; 28.085 g ÷ 4.6635 × 10⁻²³ g = 6.022 × 10²³ atoms per mole. The real spheres used their own measured values. "Molar mass" is Lesson 5.2's word; here say "the mass of one mole of silicon".

Moles to particles

The map

Moles on the left. Particles on the right. The bridge is 6.022 × 1023 per mole.

Predict

2.00 mol of copper. More or fewer than 6.022 × 1023 atoms?

Cancel

The unit mol cancels. Atoms remain.

Result

1.20 × 1024 Cu atoms. More than one mole, so more than 6.022 × 1023.

Rule

Particles = moles × 6.022 × 1023. The unit mol cancels. Always name the particle.

Check

To change moles into a number of particles, you…

  2. Bdivide by 6.022 × 1023 particles per mole

A. The unit mol cancels, and particles remain.

Reading §5.1.3: the Avogadro constant is a conversion factor; multiply by 6.022 × 10²³ particles/mol so that mol cancels. Copper and other metals are counted as atoms.

Formula units

Sodium chloride

Sodium chloride is a crystal of ions. It has no molecules.

One formula unit

One formula unit: one Na+ and one Cl−.

Predict

4.50 mol of NaCl makes 2.71 × 1024 of what?

Count

4.50 mol × 6.022 × 1023 = 2.71 × 1024 formula units.

Wrong particle

"1.51 × 1024 molecules of NaCl": right number, wrong particle.

Rule

Count a metal in atoms. Count a molecular compound in molecules. Count an ionic compound in formula units.

Check

Which particles are counted in 1 mol of NaCl?

  1. Aformula units: one Na+ and one Cl− each
  2. BNaCl molecules
  3. Csodium atoms and chlorine atoms

A. NaCl has no molecules. Its formula gives the ratio of ions.

Unit 4's formula unit: the group of ions given by the formula of an ionic compound; it states a ratio and is not a molecule. The blueprint's misconception: "2.50 mol of sodium chloride contains 1.51 × 10²⁴ molecules" has the right number (2.50 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.51 × 10²⁴) and the wrong particle.
