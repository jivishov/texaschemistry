# Universal rules for every deck (binding; set by the teacher while building 4.1 and 4.2)

The reference decks `unit4/slides-4-1.html` and `unit4/slides-4-2.html` show every rule in practice. They are the baseline for every new deck; when newer accepted Unit 4 decks (4.3, 4.4, 4.5) are on the base branch they are reference too, and the newest accepted deck wins where they differ. The pre-rebuild decks are archived under `unit4/old-slides/` and are never read or reused. A new deck copies their engine, templates, chrome, register and audits. When a rule here and an older plan differ, this file wins.

## 1. Audience and delivery
10th-grade regular and some honors chemistry, many English learners. The teacher walks the room with a Logitech R800 and never touches the laptop. Students sit up to 10 m from a 16:9 projector. The deck is one standalone HTML file: inline CSS, JS and SVG, no libraries, no raster images, Google Fonts with a system fallback that the layout does not depend on, the site's head pattern with the lesson's own title and description.

## 2. Clicker
PageDown next frame; PageUp back with the state restored exactly; F5 full screen without reload; the period key (Blank) cycles the zoom units one press each, a double press when nothing is zoomed blanks the screen, Back or Escape leaves the zoom. Every reveal, animation stage, timer, answer and zoom is one press. Keys work whatever has focus; a held key advances once. Clicking the slide counter or the section label opens the slide list; N opens notes on the laptop; Ctrl+P prints the run-sheet. The footer cue says what Next does and never leaks an answer. The cover carries the R800 strip.

## 3. Legibility and page fill
Body 44 px on the 1600 x 900 canvas, headings 64 px, nothing student-facing under 34 px, animation labels at least 36 px, shapes at least 60 px. No text ever clipped or ellipsized. No frame more than one third empty: animations fill their column, cards accumulate down the column, and a finished card shrinks to its title line only. Concept slides split 52/48, or 58/42 when the animation needs width. No overlap, ever: no two visible cards, chips, badges, labels or text lines may intersect on any frame, in either theme, after accumulation and compaction, in the footer, and inside animations (canvas labels against shapes and against each other). Content that would overlap is re-flowed, shortened or moved to the next frame, never squeezed. Light theme by default, dark theme available; both audited.

## 4. Zoom
Every piece of information is a zoom unit: the animation at its current stage and its sub-parts, every card, every check with its options and feedback line, every prompt, each table row or column, each term card, each rule card. Text units zoom by re-flow at up to 2.2x; pictures by transform. A zoomed unit is centered, fully inside the slide and above the footer, filling at least 60% of width or height. Measurements are taken only after transitions settle; the zoom-then-Next path and the animation-to-sub-part path are audited with motion on, with the viewport-and-scale matrix once per workshop.

## 5. Checks
A question frame, then the answer on Next with the correct option marked and a one-line reason. Every option is mouse-clickable: correct turns green with the reason, wrong turns red with that option's own feedback line on screen at 34 px or more. Options sit in one row (short options) or stacked, never 2 + 1; four may use 2 x 2. Feedback never replaces the question. The animation stage on screen with a check shows the check's own subject. Predict-now clicks only highlight; answers return on the predictions slide. At least eight checks per deck, each tied to an objective.

## 6. Scaffolding and lesson shape
Hook from everyday life at a kid's level (4.1: a tug of war; 4.2: reading store labels) → concept in one picture → worked example → your turn → next concept. Predict before every visible animation outcome. The rule comes before the drawing that shows it. A words slide with the lesson's terms, Spanish cognates and the color legend. A one-picture summary closes period 1; a retrieval warm-up opens period 2; I do, we do, you do before the checkpoint; checkpoint routine (think 30 s, talk 60 s, write 90 s, self-check, compare; prompt always visible) with three questions; predictions revisited with "hands up"; CER exit ticket on the driving question with the question visible while the sample shows; self-rating with review slides; a last "Next" slide naming the lesson's missions. Period count from the blueprint lesson map; period 1 ends at the natural halfway point of the objectives.

## 7. Language
Simplified textbook register with the real terms; plain word first then the term once ("pull (attraction)"); one idea per sentence, 6 to 14 words; no thesis-style sentences; Flesch-Kincaid grade never above 8 for rule cards, prompts and samples, and no sentence over 14 words anywhere student-facing; sentence frames and word banks on every writing step; Spanish cognate once per term on the words slide (from the unit reading's glossary). Numbers always carry their meaning: a label line then chips ("Electronegativity (pull on electrons)" then "H 2.20", "Cl 3.16"; "charge 2+"; "bond angle 104.5°"). Spellings and names exactly as the unit reading writes them. Honors items appear only in notes and are never assessed.

## 8. Drawing conventions
A fixed color code per unit, printed as a legend on the words slide and used identically in every deck of the unit. Unit 4's code: cation red, anion blue, electron dots violet with a "−", δ+ warm, δ− teal, metal gray, nonmetal green; CPK colors for atoms in ball-and-stick with the symbol on every sphere and the caption "Atom colors show the element, not the charge" wherever ions and atoms share a slide; intersecting translucent circles for a shared pair; "+" and "−" marks on every drawn ion and electron. Other units define their own code in the plan using the site's tokens and keep it for the whole unit. 3D with the deck's own renderer wherever it aids comprehension, with a slow turn and a fixed three-quarter view under reduced motion. Atom sizes from covalent radii (Cordero 2008) for ball-and-stick and van der Waals radii (Alvarez 2013) for space-filling; radii never set bond lengths. Data values only from the unit reading and the blueprint's data notes.

## 9. Notes and timing
Every frame has a note: minutes, what to say or ask, the prediction to collect, the misconception to listen for, and for answer frames the feedback for each wrong option. Notes sum to at most 47 minutes per period with honest per-frame minutes (0.25 per reveal, 1 per check, 1 per rule card); timers match the notes; the run-sheet prints with period totals.

## 10. Repo and blueprint
The new deck is written to `unit<U>/slides-<U>-<L>.html`; if an older deck exists at that path it is moved to `unit<U>/old-slides/` first (keeping its name) and is never read or reused. At the end, the unit blueprint changes in numbers only: deck size, period split, "Slide checks: N", the lesson's matrix rows. Nothing else in the repo changes. No commit or push without the teacher's word, and never to `main`.

## 11. Verification suites (all must pass, both themes)
walk (every press, no overflow, cue present, emptiness at most one third), back (state restored), fonts (34 px floor, 36 px on canvas), keys (clicker map and guards), readability, print, reduced motion, the lesson's chemistry audit written from the reading's data, timing, superscript gap, zoom (settle wait; matrix once per workshop), click grading, layout (no 2 + 1, no clipping, no ellipsis, and no overlap: the bounding boxes of visible text elements, cards, chips and badges must not intersect beyond 2 px, and the canvas painter records every label box and asserts the same), blueprint cross-check.
