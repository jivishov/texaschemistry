# Lesson 4.4: Molecular shapes (VSEPR) (worked example of a lesson sheet; verify every line against the reading before use)

Sources: `unit4/reading-4-4.html`, `unit4/blueprint.html` (section 4.4, data and accuracy notes, matrix rows 4.4.1 to 4.4.5). TEKS C.7(C).
Driving question: How does a Lewis structure predict the shape of a molecule in three dimensions, and why does that shape matter?
Objectives: 4.4.1 Count the electron domains around a central atom from its Lewis structure: each lone pair and each single, double or triple bond counts as one. 4.4.2 Predict the electron-domain arrangement from the number of domains (linear, trigonal planar or tetrahedral), as the arrangement with the least repulsion. 4.4.3 Classify a molecule as linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal pyramidal or bent from its Lewis structure, and tell its molecular geometry from its electron-domain arrangement. 4.4.4 Draw the five molecular geometries with wedges and dashes, and compare the ideal bond angles with the measured ones. 4.4.5 Decide whether a molecule is polar from its bond polarities and its shape, and state what the VSEPR model can and cannot predict.
Reading sections and key ideas: from the reading's §4.4.1 to §4.4.5 "Key idea" boxes, simplified per RULES section 7.
Worked examples in the reading: the "Watch / Finish it / On your own" triplets of each section.
Data the deck may use (NIST, measured kept apart from ideal): H2O 104.5°, NH3 106.7°, CH4 109.5°, CO2 and HCN 180°, CH2O 116.1° H–C–H and 121.9° H–C–O; ideal angles 180°, 120°, 109.5°; C–H (ΔEN 0.35) counts as nonpolar. Use only the molecules the reading's core sections use for each shape and for polarity.
Misconceptions: a double bond is two domains (one direction per bond); domain arrangement equals molecular shape (lone pairs are excluded from the shape name); polar bonds guarantee a polar molecule (check cancellation with the shape).
Honors boundary (notes only): polarity across all five shapes (HCN and CH2O polar, BF3 nonpolar); SO2 bent with three domains and resonance.
Out of core: formal charge, resonance, hybridization, expanded octets, five or six domains.
Blueprint models expected: a domain model (choose 2, 3 or 4 domains and 0, 1 or 2 lone pairs; domains spread; the arrangement and the molecular shape are named); turnable 3D models in ball-and-stick and space-filling views with measured angles, lone pairs and δ labels; a wedge-and-dash matching activity against a turnable model.
Missions named on the last slide: Classify the shape; Draw with wedges; Polar or nonpolar.
Size: 24 to 26 slides, 9 checks, two periods; period 1 ends after the five shapes (objective 4.4.3).
Hook at kid level: balloons tied at one knot push apart into the shapes (the classic demonstration, animated); then "why water is bent" leading to polarity.
Narrative thread: "balloons push apart; the shape decides the pull".
Color code additions: lone pairs as translucent violet lobes; dipole arrows in the δ colors.
Animations: balloons (the hook); domainModel (2, 3, 4 domains spreading to linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, then lone pairs removing atoms to give trigonal pyramidal and bent, names and ideal angles); shape3d (the reading's molecules in ball-and-stick and space-filling, lone pairs as lobes, angle arcs with measured values, δ labels); wedgeDash (a flat drawing becomes wedges and dashes while the 3D model turns to match); polarity (bond dipole arrows that cancel or add with the shape); shapeSort (your-turn classification from Lewis structures).
Spanish cognates: from the reading's glossary (electron domain, dominio de electrones; linear, lineal; trigonal planar, trigonal plana; tetrahedral, tetraédrica; trigonal pyramidal, piramidal trigonal; bent, angular; polar, polar; verify against the glossary).
