# Lesson 4.3: Covalent compounds: names and Lewis structures (worked example of a lesson sheet; verify every line against the reading before use)

Sources: `unit4/reading-4-3.html`, `unit4/blueprint.html` (section 4.3, data and accuracy notes, matrix rows 4.3.1 to 4.3.5). TEKS C.7(B), C.7(C).
Driving question: How do a molecule's name and Lewis structure show its atoms and the electrons that hold them together?
Objectives: 4.3.1 Name binary molecular compounds with the prefixes mono- to deca-, write their formulas from their names, and use the accepted names water, ammonia and methane. 4.3.2 Count the valence electrons in a molecule, and tell bonding pairs from lone pairs in a Lewis structure. 4.3.3 Draw Lewis structures with single bonds, in which each atom has an octet and each hydrogen atom has two electrons. 4.3.4 Draw Lewis structures with double or triple bonds when single bonds leave an atom short of an octet. 4.3.5 Check a Lewis structure or a name, find the error, and correct it.
Reading sections and key ideas: take from the reading's §4.3.1 to §4.3.5 "Key idea" boxes verbatim and simplify per RULES section 7; they become the rule cards.
Worked examples in the reading: the "Watch / Finish it / On your own" triplets of each section become I do, we do, you do.
Data the deck may use: valence electrons by group; prefixes mono- to deca-; the accepted names water, ammonia, methane; the reading's spelling of prefix names (for example whether it writes tetroxide or tetraoxide); no mono- on the first element.
Misconceptions: a line is one electron (count two per pair); hydrogen needs an octet (check a two-electron duet); multiple bonds add electrons (redistribute existing lone pairs).
Honors boundary (notes only): octet exceptions BF3 (six on boron), SF6 (twelve on sulfur), NO2 (odd count) and how N2O4 forms.
Out of core: formal charge, resonance, hybridization, molecular orbitals, expanded octets as core content.
Blueprint models expected: a name lab that builds a molecular name from prefix buttons and checks it against the formula; a Lewis builder that places bonding and lone pairs on a skeleton and counts electrons; a turnable 3D model beside each finished structure.
Missions named on the last slide: Name the molecule; Draw the structure; Check the drawing.
Size: 24 to 26 slides, 9 checks, two periods; period 1 ends after single-bond structures (objective 4.3.3).
Hook at kid level: prefixes students already know (bicycle, tricycle, octopus, decade) become the counting code of molecule names; a Lewis structure as a seating chart where every electron needs a seat and every atom wants a full row.
Narrative thread: "the counting code, then the seating chart".
Color code additions: bonding pair and lone pair distinguished by a shared-pair lens versus a pair on the atom's own side; CPK atoms in 3D.
Animations: prefixCode (everyday prefixes to mono- through deca-); nameLab (prefix tiles snap onto element roots, mono- dropped on the first element, the three accepted names); electronCount (valence electrons per atom from the group, summed, "two per pair"); lewisBuilder (skeleton, bonding pairs placed, lone pairs placed, octet and duet checks lighting up, a counter of electrons used); multiBond (a lone pair slides into the bond to make a double or triple bond, total unchanged: CO2, N2, HCN, CH2O); mol3d (the finished molecule turning beside its structure); errorSpot (wrong structures and names with the fix).
Spanish cognates: from the reading's glossary (molecule, molécula; prefix, prefijo; valence electrons, electrones de valencia; lone pair, par solitario; bonding pair, par de enlace; octet, octeto; double bond, enlace doble; verify against the glossary).
