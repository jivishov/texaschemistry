        Weighing atoms · Textbook §2.3.1

          Measuring atomic mass

          A mass spectrometer turns atoms into ions, speeds them up, and bends them with a magnet. Lighter ions bend more.

          Each isotope lands in its own place, and the detector counts how many ions arrive there.

          The atomic mass unit (u)

          1 u is one twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom, so carbon-12 is exactly 12 u.

          1 u ≈ 1.66 × 10−24 g. Proton 1.007 u, neutron 1.009 u, electron 0.0005 u.

          1. Unit · 2 min. Grams are far too large: one chlorine atom is about 6 × 10−23 g. Chemists compare every atom with carbon-12 instead, the way a balance compares a sample with a standard mass.
          2. Machine · 2 min. Walk through the four stages. F. W. Aston built an early mass spectrometer in 1919 and found isotopes in many elements (Nobel Prize, 1922). Today the same principle is used in forensic and medical labs.
          3. Check · 1 min. Carbon-12 is exact by definition, not by measurement.

            Mass number vs mass · Textbook §2.3.1

              Mass number and isotopic mass

              chlorine-353534.968853 u
              chlorine-373736.965903 u

              Key idea

              Close, but not equal. Use isotopic masses in calculations, not mass numbers.

              Why the mass is not a whole number

              A proton is 1.007 u and a neutron is 1.009 u, not exactly 1 u.

              When protons and neutrons bind into a nucleus, a little mass is released as energy, so a nucleus has less mass than its separate parts.

              1. Compare · 1 min. Ask: which column could have a decimal? Only the measured one. Mass numbers count whole particles.
              2. Why · 2 min. Keep the binding-energy idea brief; it returns in nuclear chemistry. The point for today: mass number ≈ isotopic mass, but they are different quantities.
              3. Check · 1 min. Then move to the average.

              The weighted average · Textbook §2.3.2

                Modeling chlorine's isotopes

                Read the jar. Then swap atoms with + and −. Which number moves?

                Press Pick one atom. What is its mass?

                Key idea

                Each isotope's mass is weighted by its fraction of the atoms. The fractions always add up to 1.

                1. Predict · 1 min. Students commit before the jar appears. The answer is graded at step 2.
                2. Fill · 3 min. 76 chlorine-35 and 24 chlorine-37 give 35.45 u, while the plain mean stays at 35.97 u. Make the jar 50 : 50: now the two numbers agree. Ask when a plain mean would be right (only if isotopes were equally common).
                3. Pick one · 1 min. Every atom you pick is 34.968853 u or 36.965903 u. None is 35.45 u. Return to prediction 2.
                4. Key idea · 1 min. A jar holds whole atoms, so 75.76% becomes 76 atoms. A real sample holds billions of billions of atoms, so its percents are smooth, not whole-atom steps. The table's four significant figures come from precise measurement.

                Worked example 1 · Watch · Textbook §2.3.2

                  Worked example: chlorine's atomic mass

                  average = (mass1 × fraction1) + (mass2 × fraction2) + …

                  fraction = percent ÷ 100, and the fractions add up to 1

                  Chlorine-35: 34.968853 u at 75.76%.

                  Chlorine-37: 36.965903 u at 24.24%.

                  Convert75.76% → 0.7576 and 24.24% → 0.2424. Check: 0.7576 + 0.2424 = 1.

                  Multiply34.968853 × 0.7576 = 26.4924 and 36.965903 × 0.2424 = 8.9605

                  Add26.4924 + 8.9605 = 35.4529, so the average is 35.45 u, near chlorine-35.

                  Think of it this way

                  A course grade where tests count 70% and homework 30%. A 90 on tests and a 60 on homework give 0.70 × 90 + 0.30 × 60 = 81, not the plain mean of 75.

                  The limit: a teacher picks grade weights. For isotopes, nature sets the weights: the share of atoms.

                  1. Recipe · 1 min. Students copy the recipe. Each fraction is the share of atoms with that mass.
                  2. Convert · 30 s. The most common error is multiplying by 75.76 instead of 0.7576, which gives about 3,545.
                  3. Multiply · 1 min. Keep four decimal places until the end.
                  4. Add · 30 s. Matches the periodic table. Sense check: the answer lies between the two masses and near the more common one.
                  5. Analogy · 1 min. Ask students where else they meet weighted averages (GPA, sports ratings). Stress the limit: nobody chooses isotope weights.

                    Why a decimal · Textbook §2.3.3

                      What the average means

                      Chlorine: three times more chlorine-35

                      Bromine: nearly equal shares

                      No chlorine atom has the average

                      Every chlorine atom is 34.97 u or 36.97 u. 35.45 u describes the mixture, like a class average of 83.5 that no student scored.

                      Helium is 99.9999% helium-4 (4.002603 u), so its average, 4.0026 u, almost equals that mass.

                      1. Chlorine · 1 min. Dot size shows abundance. The weighted average sits near the large dot; the plain mean sits halfway.
                      2. Bromine · 2 min. Bromine is 50.69% bromine-79, so the average, 79.90 u, sits almost halfway. The rule holds: nearly equal shares give a nearly central average.
                      3. Key idea · 1 min. Analogy limit: test scores vary freely, but each atom's mass is one of a few fixed values. Helium is the extreme case: 99.9999% helium-4 at 4.002603 u, so its average, 4.0026 u, almost equals helium-4.

                      Isotope ratios · Textbook §2.3.3

                        Uses of isotope ratios

                        Natural carbon is about 98.9% carbon-12 and 1.1% carbon-13. Plants and processes shift that share very slightly, and a mass spectrometer can measure the shift.

                        Sports doping tests

                        Synthetic testosterone is made from plant sterols, which have less carbon-13 than the steroids the body makes. A low carbon-13 share in urine points to testosterone from outside the body.

                        Honey and food origin

                        Corn and cane syrup have more carbon-13 than flower nectar, so diluted honey can be detected. Other isotope ratios trace where foods such as wine were produced.

                        1. Fingerprint · 30 s. These shifts are tiny, which is why the periodic table gives some elements a range (bromine 79.901 to 79.907).
                        2. Doping · 1 min. Anti-doping labs use carbon isotope ratio mass spectrometry (Cawley and Flenker, 2008).
                        3. Food · 1 min. Related: most airport trace detectors use ion mobility spectrometry, a relative of mass spectrometry that sorts ions by how fast they drift.
                        4. Check · 30 s. Carbon-13 has 6 protons like every carbon atom; only the neutron count differs.

                          Honors: abundance from the average

                          Copper · 63.546 u

                          Synthesis · Textbook §2.3.2 and §2.3.3

                            Summary: average atomic mass

                            The recipe, with chlorine

                            1. Read each isotope's mass and percent from the spectrum. 34.968853 u, 75.76%
                            2. Change each percent to a fraction: divide by 100. 0.7576
                            3. Multiply each mass by its fraction. 26.4924 and 8.9605
                            4. Add the products. 35.45 u

                            Three checks on any answer

                            1. It lies between the lightest and heaviest isotopic masses. 34.97 < 35.45 < 36.97
                            2. It is pulled toward the most abundant isotope. near chlorine-35
                            3. The fractions add up to 1. 0.7576 + 0.2424 = 1

                            So: 35.45 is a decimal because it averages a mixture of 34.97 u and 36.97 u atoms, weighted 3 to 1.

                            1. Recipe · 1 min. Students check the recipe in their notebook against slide 9.
                            2. Three checks · 1 min. Ask which check catches the plain mean (check 2) and which catches using percents as fractions (check 1).
                            3. Answer · 30 s. This is the core of the exit ticket. Do not dwell; the CER comes on slide 20.
