2.2

Inside the atom

Doctors treat an overactive thyroid gland by giving the patient a capsule of iodine to swallow. The same element is added to table salt, which is harmless to eat. Both are iodine atoms with exactly the same number of protons. The difference lies deep inside each atom, in a particle that was not discovered until 1932.Doctors can treat an overactive thyroid with a capsule of iodine. Table salt has iodine too, and salt is harmless. Both are iodine atoms with the same number of protons. The difference is in a particle deep inside the atom, found in 1932.

§2.2.1

Finding the neutron

By 1920 two were known. Thomson had found the light, negative in 1897, and Rutherford had found the tiny positive in 1911 (Lesson 2.1). Rutherford named the nucleus's positive particle the .By 1920 scientists knew two particles inside atoms. The electron was found in 1897. The nucleus was found in 1911, and its positive particles were named protons.

One puzzle remained. A helium nucleus has a charge of +2, so it holds 2 protons, yet its mass is about 4 times a proton's. Something in the nucleus had mass but no charge. A neutral particle is difficult to detect, because electric and magnetic fields do not deflect it.There was a puzzle. Helium has 2 protons but about 4 times a proton's mass. Something else in the nucleus had mass but no charge. Neutral particles are hard to find because fields do not bend them.

In 1932 James Chadwick, working in Cambridge, England, fired alpha particles at the metal beryllium. An unknown radiation came out that fields could not bend. When it struck paraffin wax, it knocked protons out at high speed. Only a neutral particle with about the same mass as a proton could do that, much as one billiard ball sends another rolling. Chadwick identified it as the .In 1932, James Chadwick shot alpha particles at beryllium. A radiation came out that fields did not bend, so it had no charge. It knocked protons out of wax, so its mass was about a proton's. He identified it as the neutron.

Key idea

A neutron has no charge and about the same mass as a proton. Neutrons and protons together make up the nucleus.

§2.2.2

Where the particles are

Protons and neutrons are packed together in the nucleus at the center of the atom. Electrons occupy the around it. As Lesson 2.1 showed, electrons do not follow fixed paths like planets. The cloud shows the region where an electron is likely to be found.Protons and neutrons are in the nucleus, at the center. Electrons are in the electron cloud around the nucleus. Electrons do not move in fixed paths. The cloud shows where they are likely to be.

The nucleus is tiny. Its diameter is only about 1/10,000 to 1/100,000 of the atom's diameter, depending on the atom. Yet protons and neutrons each have about 1,836 times the mass of an electron, so the nucleus holds more than 99.9% of the atom's mass. The electron cloud fills almost all of the atom's volume but contributes almost none of its mass.The nucleus is very small: about 1/10,000 to 1/100,000 as wide as the atom. But it holds more than 99.9% of the mass, because protons and neutrons are so much heavier than electrons. The cloud is large but has very little mass.

Think of it this way: if an atom were a football stadium, its nucleus would be a marble at the center, holding more than 99.9% of the atom's mass. The limit: a stadium has walls, but an atom's edge is a fuzzy cloud with no sharp boundary.

Key idea

Nearly all of an atom's mass is in its tiny nucleus. Nearly all of its volume is the electron cloud.

§2.2.3

Atomic number and mass number

The number of protons in an atom's nucleus is its , written Z. The atomic number determines the . Every atom with 6 protons is carbon, and every atom with 7 protons is nitrogen. The periodic table lists the elements in order of atomic number.The atomic number, Z, is the number of protons. It tells you the element. Six protons always means carbon. Seven protons always means nitrogen. The periodic table is in order of atomic number.

The , written A, counts the particles in the nucleus: protons plus neutrons. Electrons are not counted, because they add almost no mass. If you know A and Z, you can find the number of neutrons.The mass number, A, is protons plus neutrons. Electrons are not counted because they are so light. If you know A and Z, you can find the neutrons.

mass number A = protons + neutrons · neutrons = A − Z

A has no overall charge, so it has as many electrons as protons. A neutral carbon atom has 6 electrons. Adding or removing a neutron changes the mass number but never the element.A neutral atom has the same number of electrons as protons. Neutral carbon has 6 electrons. Adding or removing a neutron changes the mass number, not the element.

Key idea

Protons determine the element. Protons plus neutrons give the mass number.

The mass number is a whole-number count of particles. It is not the average atomic mass printed on the periodic table, which you will study in Lesson 2.3.

§2.2.4

Isotopes and isotope notation

Dalton thought every atom of an element had the same mass. In 1913 J. J. Thomson found that neon is a mixture of atoms with two different masses, now called neon-20 and neon-22. The same year Frederick Soddy named such atoms . Today we know that isotopes are atoms of the same element, with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.Dalton thought all atoms of one element had the same mass. In 1913, Thomson found two kinds of neon atoms with different masses. These are isotopes: same element, same protons, different numbers of neutrons.

Isotopes at work

  • Iodine-131 The thyroid gland collects iodine from the blood to make its hormones, and it cannot tell isotopes apart. When a patient swallows radioactive iodine-131, the thyroid concentrates it, and its radiation destroys overactive or cancerous thyroid cells. Ordinary iodine-127 has 74 neutrons; iodine-131 has 78.The thyroid collects iodine and cannot tell isotopes apart. Swallowed iodine-131 gathers there, and its radiation destroys overactive or cancer cells. Iodine-127 has 74 neutrons; iodine-131 has 78.
  • Technetium-99m It is the most widely used medical isotope: about 80% of nuclear-medicine scans use it. Its nucleus emits gamma rays that a camera outside the body detects, so doctors can image bones, the heart and other organs. The m stands for metastable: the nucleus holds extra energy. It has 43 protons and 56 neutrons.It is the most used medical isotope. About 80% of nuclear-medicine scans use it. A camera detects the gamma rays it gives off, so doctors can see bones and organs. The m means the nucleus has extra energy. It has 43 protons and 56 neutrons.
  • Carbon-14 A small fraction of the carbon in every living thing is carbon-14. Because it behaves chemically like carbon-12, it enters plants and animals along with ordinary carbon. Scientists use it to date old wood and bone, a method you will study in a later unit.All living things contain a little carbon-14. It acts like carbon-12, so living things take it in. Scientists use it to find the age of old wood and bone. You will learn how in a later unit.
Honors · Hydrogen's three isotopes

Hydrogen is the only element whose isotopes have their own names. Hydrogen-1 () has no neutrons at all. Deuterium, hydrogen-2 (), has 1 neutron and is stable. Tritium, hydrogen-3 (), has 2 neutrons and is radioactive. All three have 1 proton, so all three are hydrogen.

§2.2.5

Ions: gaining and losing electrons

A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so the charges cancel. In chemical changes, atoms can lose or gain electrons. Then the charges no longer cancel, and the particle is an : an atom or group of atoms with a net charge.In a neutral atom, the numbers of protons and electrons are equal, so the charges cancel. Atoms can lose or gain electrons. Then the atom has a charge and is called an ion.

charge = protons − electrons

An atom that loses electrons has more protons than electrons, so it becomes a positive ion, a . A sodium atom (11 protons, 11 electrons) that loses one electron becomes Na⁺: 11 − 10 = +1. An atom that gains electrons becomes a negative ion, an . A chlorine atom (17 protons) that gains one electron becomes Cl⁻: 17 − 18 = −1.An atom that loses electrons becomes positive: a cation. Sodium loses 1 electron and becomes Na⁺ (11 − 10 = +1). An atom that gains electrons becomes negative: an anion. Chlorine gains 1 and becomes Cl⁻ (17 − 18 = −1).

Ions never form by gaining or losing protons. Protons are held in the nucleus, which chemical changes do not affect. If a proton were added, the atom would become a different element, not a sodium ion: an atom with 12 protons is magnesium.Ions never form by changing protons. Protons stay in the nucleus during chemical changes. Changing the protons would make a different element.

Key idea

Ions form when atoms lose or gain electrons. The protons, and so the element, stay the same.

Ions in your body

Your body fluids contain many dissolved ions, called . Sodium ions (Na⁺) and potassium ions (K⁺) carry the electrical signals in your nerves. Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) trigger the contraction of muscles, including the heart. Chloride ions (Cl⁻) help balance the water in and around your cells. Sweat carries some of these ions away, which is why sports drinks contain sodium and potassium.Your body fluids contain ions called electrolytes. Na⁺ and K⁺ carry signals in your nerves. Ca²⁺ makes muscles contract. Cl⁻ helps balance water in your body. Sweat removes some of these ions, so sports drinks add sodium and potassium back.
