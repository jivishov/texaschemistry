Atomic Structure & Theory: unit blueprint

One set of objectives drives four surfaces: a student reading, teacher slides, missions with a case file, and printable worksheets (deferred for now). Every item on every surface carries an objective ID, so alignment to TEKS C.6 and C.5(B) can be checked line by line.

Grade 10 on-level, Honors optional19 TAC §112.43(c), adopted 2020About 10 class periodsBuilt from TEKS.md wording

1. The package

A classical textbook package (reading, slides, worksheets) rebuilt as connected digital surfaces. Each surface has one job, and none of them introduces content the reading does not teach.

ReadStudent reading chapter. Book or continuous mode, checks, interactives.
TeachTeacher deck. Each slide points to a reading section.
PracticeMissions. Real data and real uses of the idea.
ApplyCase file. One real event that needs the whole unit.
PrintWorksheets and answer keys mirroring the missions (deferred).

Teacher flow, one lesson

  1. Open the deck. The driving question and predictions come first.
  2. Teach through the deck. Students follow the matching reading section or read it as homework.
  3. Students complete the stop-and-check items and the missions.
  4. Once released, the worksheet serves as notes, homework or a sub plan. The exit ticket closes the lesson.

Design rules

  • Pedagogy before engagement. Every interactive answers a question the text asks.
  • Evidence first, then the model. Students see data before they see the conclusion.
  • One objective ID per item. Missions and worksheet items never test content the reading skips.
  • "Including" items in a TEKS statement are mastered and assessed. "Such as" items are examples only.

2. Standards

Wording from TEKS.md (19 TAC §112.43(c)). The right column states what a student must produce to show mastery.

Student expectationMastery evidence
C.6(A) construct models using Dalton's Postulates, Thomson's discovery of electron properties, Rutherford's nuclear atom, Bohr's nuclear atom, and Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle to show the development of modern atomic theory over time
Builds each of the five models from evidence and explains which result forced each revision. All five are named, so all five are assessed.
C.6(B) describe the structure of atoms and ions, including the masses, electrical charges, and locations of protons and neutrons in the nucleus and electrons in the electron cloud
States the relative mass, charge and location of each particle. Determines Z, A, neutrons and ion charge from symbols and the reverse.
C.6(C) investigate the mathematical relationship between energy, frequency, and wavelength of light using the electromagnetic spectrum and relate it to the quantization of energy in the emission spectrum
Uses c = λf and E = hf from spectroscope or flame-test data. Explains discrete lines as transitions between fixed energy levels.
C.6(D) calculate average atomic mass of an element using isotopic composition
Calculates a weighted average from isotopic masses and abundances, and explains why the result is a decimal.
C.6(E) construct models to express the arrangement of electrons in atoms of representative elements using electron configurations and Lewis dot structures
Writes full and noble-gas configurations and draws Lewis dot structures for representative elements.
C.5(B) predict the properties of elements in chemical families, including alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, halogens, noble gases, and transition metals, based on valence electrons patterns using the Periodic Table
Predicts reactivity and typical ion charge from valence electrons for the five named families. Shared with Unit 3.
Science and engineering practices. These are woven into every lesson, at least 40% of instructional time per (c)(1). The ones used most in this unit:
  • 1(G) develop and use models; 1(H) distinguish hypotheses, theories and laws.
  • 2(A) advantages and limitations of models; 2(B) patterns in data; 2(C) calculations.
  • 3(A) evidence-based explanations; 3(C) scientific argumentation.
  • 4(A) critique explanations with evidence; 4(B) how past research shaped scientific thought.
Aufbau, Pauli and Hund are not named in C.6(E), so they appear as supporting ideas and Honors extensions.

Rule 1

Every objective appears on all four surfaces: a reading section, at least one slide, a mission task and a worksheet item.

Rule 2

Every wrong answer choice maps to a named misconception, so feedback addresses the reasoning, not just the answer.

Rule 3

Every slide shows its reading section number (for example §2.1.4), so teacher and textbook stay in step.

Evidence chain (the story the lesson tells)

ModelEvidence students examineWhat it explainedWhat broke it
Dalton, 1803 to 1808Mass data: carbon monoxide has 1.33 g O per 1.00 g C, carbon dioxide has 2.66 g, a 1 : 2 ratioConservation of mass, fixed composition, whole-number ratiosAtoms turned out to contain smaller charged particles
Thomson, 1897 to 1904Cathode rays bend toward the positive plate. Their charge-to-mass ratio is the same in every tube and gas, and more than 1,000 times that of a hydrogen ion.Negative particles much lighter than atoms, found in all matter. Atoms are neutral, so positive charge must exist too.Gold-foil results: a spread-out positive charge cannot bounce a fast alpha particle back
Rutherford, 1909 to 1911Geiger and Marsden: most alpha particles pass through thin gold foil. About 1 in 20,000 turn back by more than 90° (Rutherford, 1911; their 1909 platinum foil gave about 1 in 8,000).A tiny, dense, positive nucleus holding almost all the mass. The atom is mostly empty space.Could not explain why hydrogen emits only certain colors, or why electrons do not spiral inward
Bohr, 1913Hydrogen's visible line spectrum: 656, 486, 434 and 410 nmElectrons occupy fixed energy levels. A jump between levels emits one photon of one energy.Failed for atoms with more than one electron. Electrons do not follow fixed paths.
Heisenberg, 1927, and the quantum model (1925 to 1927)The uncertainty principle: position and momentum cannot both be known exactly, as a law of natureElectrons are described by probability (orbitals, the electron cloud)The current model. It is refined, not replaced.

Misconceptions this lesson targets

MisconceptionEvidence that corrects it
Old models were wrong, so they are useless.Each model correctly explained the evidence of its time. Chemists still use Dalton's idea to balance equations.
Thomson's model had protons scattered in it.Thomson pictured a spread-out sphere of positive charge, not particles. Protons were identified later.
Alpha particles bounced off electrons.Electrons are about 7,000 times lighter than an alpha particle and cannot reverse it. Only a massive, concentrated charge can.
The nucleus is a large part of the atom.Scale: the nucleus is about 1/10,000 to 1/100,000 of the atom's diameter, like a marble at the center of a football stadium.
Today's model has electrons orbiting like planets.The uncertainty principle rules out exact paths. Orbitals are regions of probability.
Uncertainty exists because our instruments are not good enough.It is a property of nature, not a measurement flaw. Better tools cannot remove it.
A theory becomes a law when it is proven.Laws describe patterns (conservation of mass). Theories explain them (atomic theory). Neither turns into the other. SEP 1(H).

7. Case file: The Sun's element

Helium was found in sunlight in 1868, found on Earth in 1895, and is still extracted from Texas natural gas today. Students act as science advisers to a Texas hospital network after the 2024 sale of the Federal Helium Reserve near Amarillo.

Driving question

Where does helium come from, and why can't a hospital simply make more?

MRI magnets are cooled by liquid helium. The federal helium program began with the Helium Act of 1925. After 1960, crude helium was stored underground in the Bush Dome reservoir of the Cliffside field, about 12 miles northwest of Amarillo. The BLM sold the Federal Helium System to Messer, which announced the closing on June 27, 2024. In 2022 three Texas Panhandle plants (Rock Creek, Sher-Han, Sunray) extracted helium from natural gas, and MRI used 18% of US helium (USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022, table 2 and figure 3). The hospital network is a made-up client, and the file says so. Built as casefile.html: a briefing, six evidence files (calculate, name the Unit 2 idea, decide), then a recommendation tab.

Evidence 1 · The yellow line · Guntur, India, Aug 18, 1868 · C.6(C)

Janssen reports a yellow line at about 587.49 nm; the file uses the modern laboratory value, 587.56 nm, and says so. Lockyer names it D3 because it sits near sodium's D lines (589.0 and 589.6 nm). Task: calculate f (5.102 × 10¹⁴ Hz) and E (3.381 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) for the new line, compare with sodium's, and decide whether the new line can be sodium.

Evidence 2 · The gas in the rock · London and Uppsala, 1895 · C.6(C) C.6(D)

Ramsay releases a gas from the uranium mineral cleveite, and its spectrum shows the same yellow line. Cleve and Langlet measure its atomic weight, close to 4. Task: compare helium's mass with hydrogen's (4.0026 ÷ 1.008 = 3.97); explain why a matching line pattern identifies an element.

Evidence 3 · Catching alpha particles · Manchester, 1908 · C.6(A)

Rutherford and Royds trap alpha particles in a sealed tube. After 2 days the helium yellow line appears faintly, and after 6 days all the strong lines are visible. Task: conclude what an alpha particle is, and connect it to the gold-foil experiment, whose probes were helium nuclei.

Evidence 4 · Inside a helium atom · C.6(B)

Build helium-4, helium-3 and the alpha particle (He²⁺). Task: find the neutrons in helium-3 (1) and the electrons in He²⁺ (0); explain why helium-3 and helium-4 are the same element (both have 2 protons).

Evidence 5 · Average atomic mass · C.6(D)

Natural helium is 99.99987% helium-4 (4.002603 u) and 0.00013% helium-3 (3.016029 u). Task: calculate the average (4.0026 u) and explain why it sits so close to 4.

Evidence 6 · Why helium will not react · C.6(E) C.5(B)

Configuration 1s², Lewis dot structure, a full outer level. Task: count valence electrons (2), match helium to the noble-gas family, and explain why it sits in group 18 rather than group 2 (its n = 1 level is full).

Final task · recommendation (CER) · SEP 3(A), 3(C)

Should the hospital network invest in a helium recovery system? The claim must cite at least three evidence files, for example:

  • Helium is an element. Chemical reactions rearrange atoms but cannot change proton number, so no reaction can make it (2.1.1, C.6(B)).
  • It forms no compounds that could hold it (C.5(B)).
  • Earth's supply builds up underground from alpha particles over geologic time (context from Evidence 3).
Case-file boundary. Alpha decay is context only; nuclear equations are assessed in Unit 11 (C.14). MRI physics and boiling points are not assessed. Historical data are simplified for clarity, and each simplification is labeled in the file.
