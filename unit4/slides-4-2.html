Charges from the group

Where are the metals? Where are the nonmetals?

Group 1 metals lose 1 electron: charge 1+.

Group 2 metals lose 2 electrons: charge 2+.

Aluminum loses 3 electrons: charge 3+.

Group 17 gains 1 electron: charge 1−. The name ends in -ide.

Group 16 gains 2 electrons: charge 2−.

Nitrogen gains 3 electrons: charge 3−.

Metal atoms lose 1, 2 or 3 valence electrons.

Nonmetal atoms gain electrons to reach eight.

A monatomic anion name ends in -ide.

Check: Which ion does magnesium form?

  1. AMg+
  2. BMg2+
  3. CMg2−

B. Magnesium is in group 2. It loses 2 electrons.

Group number gives the charge for groups 1, 2, 13 (aluminum), 15, 16 and 17. Groups 14 and 18 form no simple ions in this course. Monatomic anions take -ide: fluoride, chloride, bromide, iodide, oxide, sulfide, nitride.

Balancing the charges

Model

Every + stud needs a − socket. The total charge must be zero.

Ca and Cl

Ca2+ has two studs, so it needs two Cl− ions. The charges 2+ and 2− add to zero.

Al and O

Al3+ and O2−: how many of each ion make the total charge zero?

Al and O

Two Al3+ ions give a charge of 6+. Three O2− ions give 6−. The total charge is zero.

Rule

The total charge of an ionic compound is zero. The formula shows the smallest whole-number ratio of ions.

Check

Na+ with O2− balances as…

  1. ANaO
  2. BNaO2
  3. CNa2O

C. Two Na+ give 2+. One O2− gives 2−.

Ion-tile model (blueprint): a tile is as long as its charge. Studs equal sockets means the total charge is zero. Reading §4.2.2 Key idea: find the smallest numbers of cations and anions whose charges add to zero.

The crisscross shortcut

Shortcut

Crisscross is a fast way to balance the charges.

Mg and O

Mg2+ and O2−: cross the charge numbers again.

Cross

Each charge number becomes the other ion's subscript. The signs drop.

Check

Two Al3+ ions give 6+. Three O2− ions give 6−. The total charge is zero.

Reduce

Crossing gives Mg2O2. Divide both counts by 2: MgO.

Rule

Crisscross is a shortcut, not the final formula. Always reduce the ratio, then check the total charge.

Check

Crisscross gives Ca2O2. The formula is…

  1. ACa2O2
  2. BCaO
  3. CCaO2

B. Reduce 2 : 2 to 1 : 1. The charges 2+ and 2− make zero.

The crisscross is a shortcut for the tile picture; it must be followed by reducing and by checking the charge totals.

Naming binary compounds

Two parts

A name has two parts: the cation first, then the anion.

AnionName
F−fluoride
Cl−chloride
Br−bromide
I−iodide
O2−oxide
S2−sulfide
N3−nitride

Rule

Name the cation first, then the anion root plus -ide. Common names omit prefixes because the ion charges determine the ratio.

Check

Name CaBr2.

  1. Acalcium bromine
  2. Bcalcium bromide
  3. Ccalcium dibromide

B. The anion takes -ide. No prefix is needed.

A binary compound has exactly two elements. Common names use no prefixes (di-, tri-) because the charges fix the ratio. IUPAC also accepts a compositional name with prefixes (calcium dibromide), but the common name is calcium bromide.

The Stock system

Two irons

Iron(II) and iron(III) are two different ions, like version numbers.

Same anion

A different numeral gives a different formula: FeCl3 and FeCl2.

One ion only

Silver and zinc have one ion each. Their names have no numeral.

Work back

Start from the anion's charge. The metal must cancel it.

Driving question

The copper ion's charge determines the formula. The Roman numeral records that charge in the name.

Check

Name FeCl2.

  1. Airon(I) chloride
  2. Biron(II) chloride
  3. Ciron chloride

B. Two Cl− ions give 2−, so iron is 2+.

Stock system (Alfred Stock, 1919): the Roman numeral is the charge of the metal ion, found by working back from the anion. Only metals with more than one common ion take one. Red Cu₂O is copper(I) oxide; black CuO is copper(II) oxide: this answers slide 3.

Polyatomic ions

Atom colors show the element, not the charge.

A squad

A polyatomic ion is a group of atoms with one shared charge.

Six polyatomic ions

ammoniumNH4+
hydroxideOH−
nitrateNO3−
carbonateCO32−
sulfateSO42−
phosphatePO43−

Pattern

An -ate ion contains oxygen. Nitrate is not nitride. Sulfate is not sulfide.

Memory card

Nick the Camel ate Supper in Phoenix.

Consonants count O atoms. Vowels give the charge.

Check

Sulfate, SO42−: which statement is right?

  1. AEach O is an oxide ion.
  2. BIt is the same as sulfide.
  3. CThe whole group carries 2−.

C. The charge belongs to the whole group.

Six core polyatomic ions. Memory sentence: Nick = NO₃⁻ (3 consonants N, c, k; 1 vowel), Camel = CO₃²⁻ (3 consonants, 2 vowels), Supper = SO₄²⁻ (4 consonants, 2 vowels), Phoenix = PO₄³⁻ (4 consonants, 3 vowels). Ammonium NH₄⁺ is the only common positive one; hydroxide OH⁻.

Parentheses

Squad

OH− is one squad: one O and one H with one charge.

Two squads

Two ammonium squads ride in parentheses. One sulfate squad needs none.

One squad

One nitrate squad, so no parentheses.

Rule

Treat a polyatomic ion as one unit. Never change the subscripts inside a polyatomic ion.

Check

Write the formula of magnesium nitrate.

  1. AMgNO3
  2. BMg(NO3)2
  3. CMgN2O6

B. Mg2+ needs two NO3− squads.

Reading §4.2.4 Key idea: treat a polyatomic ion as one unit; put it in parentheses when the formula needs more than one of it; never change the subscripts inside it (SO₃²⁻ is a different ion, sulfite).
